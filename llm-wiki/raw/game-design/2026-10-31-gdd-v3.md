# Kitty: Core Awakening — Game Design Document v3

*An AROS-native isometric puzzle-platformer about concurrency, built on the team's AROS Vulkan stack (lavapipe baseline, NVK hardware tier) and developed LLM-first. The rationale for every change from v1 is in `kitty_core_awakening_review.md`. v3 (2026-10-31) changes only the presentation rules; the visual detail lives in `kitty_art_bible.md` (v2).*

### Changes from v2

The owner decided on 2026-10-31 that graphics quality improves as the player goes through the game, from period low resolution in Ch1 to modern rendering in Ch4. This restores v1's "the engine visibly evolves with the story". It changes:

- §3.2: each chapter has a **look level** (L1–L4), not just a palette.
- §4: rewritten. One look per chapter replaces one look for the whole game. Device tiers (lavapipe/NVK) are independent of look levels.
- §4.1: budgets per look level. The "≤ 6 shaders" rule is replaced.
- New §4.2: cutscenes and movie clips.
- §5.1 WINDOWS, §7 hub, §8.2 tools, §8.4 render CI, §11.2 M3/M5/M6, §12 art load, §13 D8–D9.

Unchanged: gameplay, rooms and puzzles are identical on both device tiers; NVK is never required; fixed camera and flip-screen rooms; everything in §5.3–§5.4.

## 0. The one-paragraph pitch

The System froze. Five Kickstart modules are stuck in a **circular wait**, each holding what another needs. Kitty, the init process, wakes in swap space and descends through five dormant CPU Cores. Each Core teaches one real concurrency idea as a puzzle verb: **Forbid, Fork, Join, Mutex, Semaphore**. Kitty can't break the deadlock alone, and forking harder makes it worse. She breaks it by imposing order, with help from the Contribs, the processes written by everyone else. Warm reboot. Credits roll over a Workbench that multitasks again.

**Design thesis (unchanged):** the story *teaches* the operating system, the world *celebrates* the culture, and the game is built, like AROS, by many hands.

## 3. Story

### 3.1 Premise (corrected)

AROS-land has always run on one Core, and run *beautifully*. Exec time-sliced hundreds of processes with preemptive multitasking, the way it has since 1985. The citizens never queued. They shared.

Then the **Great Panic**. A process called **Deadlock** whispered to five Kickstart modules, and each grabbed one resource and waited for the next: Exec holds the Scheduler and waits for Intuition's Screens; Intuition holds the Screens and waits for Graphics' blitter; Graphics holds the blitter and waits for DOS's disk; DOS holds the disk and waits for Layers' clip-rects; Layers holds the clip-rects and waits for Exec. **A circular wait.** Nothing crashed. Everything just *stopped*.

Deadlock isn't a monster. It's a shape: the cycle itself, given a face.

Kitty, PID 1 and the init process, is the only process that doesn't need any of those five resources to run. She wakes in swap space. Beneath the System lie five dormant **Cores**, corrupted when the Kickstart ROM was. Each Core holds one idea the System needs to survive having more than one thing happening at once.

### 3.2 Structure: one spine, five chapters

Each chapter is one Core, teaches one concept, frees one module, and has its own era **look level** (§4). The look improves chapter by chapter: that progression *is* the "engine visibly evolves" beat.

| Ch | Core | Concept & verb | Module freed | Look level | Era look & sound |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **68k Core** (the Ancestor) | Multitasking & **FORBID** | Exec | L1 | Kickstart 1.3: 320×256, 4-color blue/white/black/orange, flat shading, Paula samples |
| 2 | **PPC Core** (the Mirror) | **FORK** | Intuition | L2 | Workbench 2.x gray-blue, 640×256 hi-res, 3D bevels |
| 3 | **x86 Core** (the Foundry) | **JOIN** (barriers) | Graphics | L3 | MagicWB-style 8-color plus ramps, 640×512, dithered smooth shading, richer samples |
| 4 | **ARM Core** (the Garden) | **MUTEX** & lock ordering | DOS | L4 | Modern AROS (Zune/Wanderer): true colour, real light, soft gradients |
| 5 | **SMP Heart** | **SEMAPHORE** & the finale | Layers | all | Every earlier look at once, one per region of the screen; the warm reboot resolves them all to L4 |

**Chapter notes**
- **Ch1 (68k).** Teaches that one Core can still run many tasks. Platforms and patrols are "tasks" that time-slice visibly (they tick in turn). FORBID stops task switching: everything freezes except Kitty. It's a real Exec call, and its real cost becomes the mechanic. Hold it too long and the System starves: the screen bleeds toward a Guru Meditation, and at the limit Kitty is thrown back a Nine-Lives step. Lesson: *Forbid is powerful and rude*.
- **Ch2 (PPC, the Mirror).** Amiga history literally forked, and both branches kept going. The Mirror Core is two reflected worlds that diverged and **are both alive**. This is a tribute to every branch of the family, never "the lost path". Kitty learns FORK here. Writing tone: respectful to AmigaOS 4 and MorphOS users. Nobody is the villain.
- **Ch3 (x86, the Foundry).** Industrial scale, coolant, enormous machines that need several Kitties at once. JOIN gates teach that parallel work must *meet up* before it can continue.
- **Ch4 (ARM, the Garden).** Quiet and minimal. Low-power: rooms have a small energy budget, so every clone costs light. MUTEX pedestals, then the first two-lock rooms. Clones can deadlock *each other* here, and the room shows it. Lesson: *take locks in a fixed order*.
- **Ch5 (SMP Heart).** SEMAPHOREs (N-holder gates), then the finale.

### 3.3 The finale (rewritten)

1. **Kitty tries the v1 ending**: fork across every Core and swarm the cycle. The game lets her, and it **gets worse**. Every new Kitty joins the wait queue, the cycle grows another link, and the music thins channel by channel. *More threads don't fix a deadlock.*
2. **The Archivist's README** (found earlier, unexplained) now makes sense: *"Number your locks. Take them in order. Nobody waits in a circle if everybody walks the same way."*
3. **The Contribs arrive**: every disk, README author and gadget collected. Each Contrib is a process that offers to **hold one resource in the agreed order** so the modules can release theirs.
4. **The final room is a lock-ordering puzzle** using every verb. Kitty assigns Contribs to pedestals in ascending order (Forbid to buy time, Fork/Join to be in two places, Mutex to claim, Semaphore to let exactly the right number through). When the order holds, the cycle has no edge to close, and Deadlock simply has nothing left to be.
5. **Warm reboot.** The real Ctrl-Amiga-Amiga flash. All modules resident, all Cores online. Everything the player saved stays saved.

One climax, the right lesson, and the moral lands without a speech: *no single process saves an operating system, and more of the same process doesn't either.*

### 3.4 Characters

- **Exec**: the Scheduler. Proud, fast, frozen mid-time-slice. Freed in Ch1, then narrates the time-slicing tutorial.
- **Intuition**: the Blind Oracle. Arranges all Screens and Windows; can't see herself, sees through everything else. Gives the Window verbs.
- **Graphics**: the vain painter, hoarding blitter time. Comic relief; the Ch3 rival.
- **DOS**: the archivist-librarian, keeper of the disk shelves.
- **Layers**: the quiet one who keeps the world from tearing when Windows overlap. Met exactly once, in Ch5. The last link in the cycle, and the one who forgives it.
- **The Archivist**: keeper of the Public Domain Library. Speaks only in Aminet `.readme` headers (`Short:`, `Author:`, `Type:`, `Requires:`). Original character, no catchphrase borrowed from anywhere.
- **The User**: never seen, always felt. The sky flickers with their presence; the oldest citizens speak of them in lowercase. (Generic computing folklore, owned by no one.)
- **Citizens: the Nybbles.** Small four-bit folk who walk in pairs to make a byte. Their routines are tasks, and you can watch them time-slice in Ch1.
- **The previous Kitty**: an earlier init process. Its READMEs are scattered everywhere. It sets up the post-game Second-Kitty arc.

## 4. Presentation (the look evolves with the story)

Two things are independent:

- **The look level** comes from the chapter (§3.2). It only goes up as the player progresses.
- **The device tier** comes from the startup self-test (§8.3). Most players render on **lavapipe**, a CPU rasterizer; a minority have **NVK** on a supported NVIDIA card.

Both tiers show the same look level in each chapter. NVK spends its headroom on resolution, frame rate and anti-aliasing, never on a different look or different content. Gameplay, rooms and puzzles are identical on both tiers, and every look level must run on lavapipe.

### Look levels (values proposed; art bible §2 has the full table)

| | L1 Dawn (Ch1) | L2 Bevel (Ch2) | L3 Foundry (Ch3) | L4 Modern (Ch4) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Internal resolution, baseline | 320×256 | 640×256 (2× vertical) | 640×512 | 640×512 |
| Internal resolution, enhanced | 320×256 | 640×256 | 640×512 | window resolution, up to 1920×1080 |
| Colour | palette, 4 core colours | palette ≤ 16 | palette ≤ 24 | true colour |
| Shading | flat | flat, bevels | Gouraud dithered back to the palette | per-pixel light |
| Transparency | ordered dither | ordered dither | ordered dither | alpha blend |

**Ch5** composites the earlier levels, one per screen region, into an L4 frame. **The hub** uses the level of the highest completed chapter (§7).

### Device tiers

| | Baseline (lavapipe) | Enhanced (NVK) |
|---|---|---|
| Internal resolution | the level's baseline value | the level's enhanced value |
| Frame rate | 25 fps | 50 fps |
| Anti-aliasing | none | 4× MSAA on L4 |
| Selection | Automatic: NVK if a Turing+ device enumerates and passes a startup self-test, else lavapipe. Override via `ENV:Kitty/Renderer` | |

### Rules on every level

- **Camera:** fixed-angle isometric (dimetric) 3D, **flip-screen rooms**, in the Amiga isometric tradition. No player-controlled camera. Each room fits one view, so the whole puzzle is always visible, clones included. The puzzle safe area is 5:4 on every level (open decision D8 for L4 widescreen margins).
- **Each room is a Screen.** Entering a room is literally `OpenScreen`. The title bar shows the Screen name, and the depth gadget in the corner returns to the hub.
- **Readability first.** Every gameplay object keeps one silhouette across all levels. A higher level adds detail, never clutter, and every room must also read when rendered at L1.
- **Colour:** L1–L3 use a per-chapter palette, enforced by `kitty-pal` at build time: each chapter literally can't use colours its era didn't have. L4 uses true colour inside a per-room colour script, checked at build time. The System's own colours (Guru red/black, clone stripes, UI highlight) are the same in every chapter.
- **Light** comes from the top-left on every level: baked into palette indices on L1–L3, a real key light on L4.
- **Frame rate:** 25 fps target, 50 fps stretch (PAL field rates). Simulation is always 50 Hz fixed; rendering interpolates. The look level is render-only and never changes the state hash.
- **Kitty:** low-poly, expressive, squash-and-stretch, authored-key tail. One rig on every level, with a mesh variant per level. Clones carry a per-clone colour stripe on the scarf: dithered on L1–L3, translucent with a stripe rim light on L4.
- **Death:** "Cat Meditation", a red-bordered alert box identical on every level, then the Nine-Lives rewind.

### 4.1 Performance budgets (provisional until M0 for L1, until M3 for L4)

| Budget | Provisional | Set by |
|---|---|---|
| Triangles per frame | L1/L2 3,000; L3 6,280; L4 17,000 | M0 spike (L1), M3 L4 test room |
| On-screen Kitties (player + clones) | 6 | M0 spike |
| Shaders | a pipeline set per look level, all created at startup or chapter load, never inside a room; L1 keeps v2's ≤ 6 (1 VS, ≤ 5 FS) | Design rule, engine rev 6 |
| Overdraw | ≤ 2× | Isometric layout + back-face culling |
| Frame time, baseline (lavapipe) on the reference target | ≤ 40 ms on every level | D4 reference hardware |
| Frame time, enhanced (NVK) | ≤ 20 ms on every level | D4 reference card |
| Resident memory incl. Vulkan library | ≤ 512 MB | Lavapipe alone is ~130 MB |

All budgets are set against **the baseline**.

- If M0 misses the L1 budgets on lavapipe, the order of fallbacks is: cut texture sampling, then drop to 320×200, then cap clones at 4, then make AROS-side work (lavapipe thread count, SMP).
- If L4 misses 40 ms on lavapipe, the order is: bloom and fog, then point lights down to 1, then internal resolution down to 480×384, then triangles down to the L3 budget.
- Never fall back to a free camera, never cut readability, and never make NVK required.

### 4.2 Cutscenes and movie clips

The engine plays both (art bible §8):

- **In-engine cutscenes** (the default): scripted sequences on the replay/input-log path, rendered at the chapter's look level. They are deterministic, identical on both tiers, re-skin automatically and cost almost no disk. Each chapter opens with one that upgrades the look from the previous level.
- **Movie clips:** pre-rendered, for what rooms can't show (boot intro, warm-reboot flash, credits). L1–L3 clips are palette-indexed and delta-compressed, ANIM-style. L4 clips are true colour in an open codec (open decision D9). Each clip ≤ 60 s; all clips together ≤ 32 MB in 1.0; decoding fits the 40 ms baseline frame.
- Both are skippable, subtitled from the locale catalogs, and never the only place gameplay information appears. A clip that shows Kitty or AROS branding ships with a Process 1 variant (§2).

## 5. Mechanics

### 5.1 The verb set (8 verbs, all real OS concepts)

| Verb | Real concept | How it plays | Unlocked |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Move / Jump** | — | Standard isometric platforming | Ch1 |
| **FORBID** | `Forbid()`/`Permit()`: stop task switching | Hold fire on a **keyboard node** (cat on keyboard): all tasks freeze, Kitty moves. A starvation meter fills, and overflow forces a rewind | Ch1 |
| **NINE LIVES** | Saved state / checkpoint | Death rewinds up to 9 s along the snapshot ring. Lives refill at checkpoints | Ch1 |
| **FORK** | fork(): a second thread from the current state | Step on a **Fork Pad**: the world pauses, and Kitty records a thread of up to 10 s. On commit, a clone replays that thread *on its own Core* from the fork moment, while Kitty continues from the same moment | Ch2 |
| **JOIN** | join / barrier: wait for threads to finish | **Join Gates** open only when every live thread has arrived. Arriving threads merge back, and their carried items transfer to Kitty | Ch3 |
| **MUTEX** | Mutual exclusion lock | **Lock Pedestals** hold one token. A bridge or door is solid only while its lock is held. Two-lock rooms teach ordering: clones that take locks in opposite order deadlock visibly, and the room shows the wait arrows | Ch4 |
| **SEMAPHORE** | Counting semaphore | **Semaphore Gates** display N. Up to N threads pass, and the N+1th waits until one leaves | Ch5 |
| **WINDOWS** | Intuition depth arrangement | Grab a Window panel. **Back** = ghost (pass-through; dithered on L1–L3, translucent on L4), **Front** = solid floor or wall. Gained when Intuition is freed (after Ch2), then used everywhere | Ch2→ |
| **REQUESTERS** | System requesters | Doors are dialogs. `[Retry]` resets the room. `[Cancel]` removes the obstacle **and closes something behind you**. Cancel is always tempting and always costly | Ch1→ |

FORK's in-world framing should say what it is, not just do it. Fork Pad text: *"A fork copies you as you are right now. From here, you are two."* The recording is how the player *programs* the second thread. That's honest abstraction, not a mislabel.

### 5.2 Controls (joystick-first, honestly)

One fire button plus directions must be **sufficient** to finish the game:
- Most verbs are **places** (pads, pedestals, gates, keyboard nodes, requester buttons). Walking onto them and pressing fire is the whole interaction.
- **Tap fire** = jump. **Hold fire on a node** = Forbid, grab Window, or confirm Fork.
- **Hold fire anywhere for 0.5 s** = a 4-way **pie menu** (up = Rewind, down = Window depth toggle, left/right = cycle selected thread). This is the only modal UI.
- Keyboard and a 2-button pad map the pie actions to direct keys. That's an enhancement, never a requirement.

### 5.3 Verb interaction rules

Recorded clones make every pairwise interaction a potential desync. These rules are normative, and each one gets a test.

| Interaction | Rule |
|---|---|
| Rewind × clones | Rewind restores the **whole world snapshot**, clones included. Clones are world state, not overlays |
| Forbid × clones | Clones are tasks, so Forbid freezes them too. Kitty alone moves |
| Window depth × clone recording | Clones replay **inputs**, not positions. If a Window changed, the clone's run diverges (by design), and the room may make that the puzzle |
| Requester Retry × clones | Retry kills every thread and restores the room's entry snapshot |
| Requester Cancel × clones | Cancel is world state, so clones see the closed path |
| Fork during Forbid | Not allowed. The pad greys out: you can't create a task with switching disabled |
| Join with an unfinished clone | Gate waits. If a clone's thread ends away from the gate, it idles in place ("zombie") until the room is retried. That's a lesson, not a bug |
| Clone death | Clone despawns with a small Guru flash. Gates waiting on it never open, so the room needs Retry |

### 5.4 The snapshot system (one system, six features)

A 50 Hz fixed-point simulation with a ring buffer of world snapshots and an input log powers:
1. Nine Lives rewind
2. FORK recordings (an input slice + start snapshot)
3. Replay/demo mode (attract mode in the hub)
4. **Recordings as files** in `SYS:`-style directories, shareable by sneakernet
5. Saves (snapshot + progress, written as a visible directory tree)
6. CI: same input log → same state hash, on Linux and on AROS

Build this first (M1). Everything else sits on it.

## 7. The Hub: Workbench

The hub is a small isometric Workbench neighbourhood that fills up as chapters complete. It uses the look level of the highest completed chapter and upgrades in place each time a chapter is finished:
- Freed modules move in (Exec's clock tower, Intuition's window shop, …).
- Found disks become arcade cabinets.
- Rescued Nybbles walk simple scripted routines, enough for life without a Sims system.
- The **Shell** kiosk: an earned CLI. Real AROS commands do real things in-world: `cd` fast-travels between chapter Screens, `list` shows what's in a room, `loadwb` resets the hub, `assign` creates a shortcut. It doubles as a gentle tutorial for actual AROS commands.
- The **Prefs drawer**: settings are literally `ENV:`/`ENVARC:` variables, editable from the Shell.

The hub is deliberately **the seed of the post-launch playground (1.3)**. It has residents, places and routines, but no needs, no economy and no build mode. 1.3 adds those without rewriting 1.0.

## 8. Engine & technical stack

### 8.1 Decision: small custom engine, C++20 subset

Port nothing. Godot/Bevy/O3DE on AROS would be a second project larger than the game. The engine stays small (target ≤ 20k lines) so LLM-driven changes stay reviewable.

**Language rules** (enforced by compiler flags and a lint script):
- C++20, with `-fno-exceptions -fno-rtti`; no `std::thread`, `<iostream>`, `<filesystem>` or `<regex>`.
- No global objects with non-trivial constructors. Initialize explicitly from `main`.
- All OS access goes through `platform/` (one interface, two implementations: `linux/` and `aros/`).
- Gameplay simulation uses **fixed-point (Q16.16) and integer math only**. No libm calls in `sim/`. Floats are allowed only in `render/` and `audio/`.

### 8.2 Stack

```
C++20 subset
├── platform/aros     Intuition window via vulkan_loader.library (vkCreateArosSurfaceKHR),
│                     input via vkPollArosEventsKHR + lowlevel.library joystick,
│                     timer.device frame pacing (NEVER WaitTOF), AHI audio,
│                     locale.library catalogs, DOS file I/O
├── platform/linux    SDL2 (window, input, audio), POSIX files
├── Vulkan 1.3 core   raw dispatch table loaded via vkGetInstanceProcAddr from the
│                     stock Khronos vulkan_loader.library (ICDs found by manifest:
│                     vulkan_lavapipe.library, vulkan_nvk.library); no volk, no
│                     Vulkan-Hpp; no vendor extensions; only features both
│                     lavapipe and NVK expose
├── glm               render-side math only
├── modplay           own ProTracker replayer (~1.5k lines), per-channel mute, deterministic
├── Dear ImGui        Linux debug builds only; never compiled for AROS
└── tools/            level compiler, puzzle solver, palette quantizer (L1–L3), colour-script
                      checker (L4), state-hash checker (host)
```

### 8.3 Rendering rules for the AROS Vulkan stack

The AROS paths are young and some failures are silent, so the engine is strict by construction:
- Every Vulkan create-info is zero-initialized and **spec-complete**. Optional state structs (rasterization, depth-stencil, color-blend, dynamic) are always supplied explicitly.
- Per frame: **acquire → record → submit → present**, with no exceptions.
- Handle arrays are 8-byte stride, and NDC is standard Vulkan (z ∈ [0,1]).
- A renderer integration test reads back the presented image and checks it against a golden image. "No error returned" proves nothing.
- Back-face culling is set explicitly per pipeline.
- **Open the Vulkan library once at startup and keep it open until exit.** Lavapipe is ~130 MB resident, and the current boot lane crashes when a resident library is force-expunged. Never call or trigger `Avail FLUSH`.
- **Startup self-test** before choosing a tier: create a device, render a 64×64 test scene, compare it to the stored golden image. On any mismatch or error, fall back to lavapipe and log why to `T:Kitty.log`.
- Pin the Mesa version per release. The AROS lavapipe/NVK builds and the host CI build must come from the same Mesa commit.

### 8.4 Verification pipeline (CI, every commit)

1. **Linux build + unit tests** (sim, snapshot, modplay, level compiler).
2. **State-hash replay suite:** every recorded input log in `tests/replays/` must reproduce its stored final hash.
3. **Puzzle solver:** every room in `levels/` must be solvable within its par, and must **not** be solvable by any recorded "cheese" pattern or with fewer verbs than intended.
4. **Render CI on host lavapipe**, built from the **same Mesa commit** as the AROS build: `glslangValidator` on every shader, then the scripted demo frames rendered and compared against golden images, with goldens per look level. The project already shows the Linux oracle of the same Mesa source matches the AROS guest bit for bit, which makes this a near-exact proxy for the baseline tier. (A generic GPU-driver pass alone proves nothing about AROS.)
5. **Linux Vulkan validation layers** clean on a scripted 60-second demo.
6. **AROS cross-build** with the mandatory flag set (Appendix A). Gate: `objdump -d | grep -c 'mov %rax,%r12'` == 0 on the shipped binary.
7. **On-AROS smoke run** (per milestone, via the spike agent): boot, run the demo replay on lavapipe, upload the state hash, frame-time log and final frame. **The hash must equal the Linux hash, and the frame must match the host-lavapipe golden image.**
8. **NVK card-lane run** (per milestone from M3, when a boot is granted): the same replay on NVK. The state hash must match. The frame is compared to the host NVK oracle.

## 9. Contributions: the Contribs (1.1, post-launch)

*AROS's source tree has a `contrib` directory where community ports live. The game borrows the culture: honestly, at sneakernet scale, with no server.*

### 9.1 In-fiction (in 1.0)
The Contribs are the guild who kept patching through the Panic. In 1.0 they're story characters and the finale's agents. The AUTHORS scroll in the hub lists the dev team and every in-world disk author.

### 9.2 1.1 scope: data-only contribution packs
- **What can be contributed:** rooms (made with the same level editor the developers use), room packs as new "disks", `.mod` music for the hub jukebox, locale catalogs (`.ct`), README lore.
- **What can't:** native code of any kind. AROS has no memory protection, so native code from a stranger *is* the whole machine. Scripted content waits for a purpose-built interpreter with no FFI and hard step and memory limits (a 1.x+ item, only if demand exists).
- **Package:** an Aminet-style `.lha` holding a drawer:
  ```
  FF-2001_garden_rooms/
    FF-2001_garden_rooms.readme   # Aminet header: Short/Author/Uploader/Type/Version/License
    manifest.json                 # schema-validated; license field mandatory
    rooms/  music/  catalogs/
    SHA256SUMS                    # integrity only — not a signature, and it doesn't claim to be
  ```
- **Validate:** dropping a disk into `Contrib/` runs the validator: schema, resource limits, license present, and **every room passes the puzzle solver**. Failures show a **Guru Meditation with the exact file and line**, so the error screen is the build log.
- **Distribute:** Aminet, the project's git repo, forums, USB sticks. "Curation" is a plain-text index of recommended disks, maintained in the repo. There's no voting, reputation server or revocation server. Unlisting a disk from the index is the only moderation, and it's honest about being only that.
- **Credit:** installed disks add their authors to the AUTHORS scroll. Scarf stripes count *rooms you've solved* from contributed disks, a cosmetic reward for playing other people's work.

## 10. Flavor & small systems

Kept from v1 because they're cheap and native:
- **Saves as a visible filesystem:** a real directory tree under `PROGDIR:Saves/`, copyable and tradeable. Kitty saves herself to disk.
- **ENV:/ENVARC: settings.**
- **The Shell** (see §7).
- **Recordings as files:** shareable replays, which is the demoscene catnip. The attract mode plays curated ones.
- **Benchmark mode:** `SYS:benchmark` plays a fixed replay with a demo-style camera path and prints frame-time stats. It's useful to the AROS ICD team as a real-world workload, which makes it the actual "drink your own champagne" item.
- **Demo release:** the first chapter as an `.lha` on Aminet, with a proper `.readme`. (Drop the `.adf` floppy claim: an x86-64 build won't fit or boot from one.)
- **Localization:** `locale.library` catalogs from day one. EN + DE in 1.0. FR/IT/SV are invited as community `.ct` files. Writing budget: assume 1.5× wordcount effort per language for pun-heavy text.

Cut from v1: AREXX macro recording (replays cover it), WHDLoad pack format (it's an m68k convention, and on x86 AROS it reads as a wink nobody can use), network visits, leaderboard, SMP job system as a milestone (see §11.2 M6).

## 11. Milestones

### 11.1 Operating rules
- One milestone at a time. A milestone is done when **all machine gates and its human gate pass**.
- Machine gates: CI §8.4 is green, plus the milestone's specific checks.
- Human gates are listed per milestone. Fun isn't machine-verifiable.
- **Moveset frozen from end of M1 until 1.0**, except via an explicit design change recorded in this document.
- LLMs may not refactor code that lacks tests. Write the test first.
- Verify every build output by timestamp or hash, never by `make`'s exit code.
- Commit after every milestone; tag it.

## Appendix A — AROS build requirements

Mandatory for every AROS artifact (library ABI consistency, stack protector unsupported):
```
-mcmodel=large -mno-red-zone -mno-ms-bitfields -fno-strict-aliasing
-ffixed-r12 -fno-stack-protector -fno-exceptions -fno-rtti -ffp-contract=off
```
- Cross-compile against the **same SDK tree** the target AROS image was built from (build-pc). Gate: zero `mov %rax,%r12` in shipped binaries.
- Simulation determinism: no `-ffast-math`, `-ffp-contract=off` on both platforms, no libm in `sim/`.
- After installing a rebuilt `.library` on AROS, **reboot**. Never rely on `Avail FLUSH` to swap a resident library.
- Diagnostics on AROS go through `bug()`/`RawPutChar`, or a file sink. Long traces are rate-gated so they don't wrap the debug ring.
- Networking isn't required by anything in the game. (AROSTCP non-blocking connect and IPv6 paths are known-fragile.)
